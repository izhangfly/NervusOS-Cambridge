**What We Want to Solve**
Nowadays, most robots simply have their actions hard-coded as scripts, performing on stages or addressing specific needs. The ecosystem issues are even more troublesome. Each manufacturer has its own SDK and protocol, making it easy for robots to be bought home and then have their capabilities fixed from the day of manufacture. NervusOS aims to define a unified SDK, allowing manufacturers to build the bodies, developers to write applications, and users to download them themselves.

**Progress**
At AdventureX 2026 five-day hackathon, we built a working prototype: 8 repositories, approximately 87,000 lines of code, using Go and Kotlin. The core includes a complete three-layer security path, control arbitration, permission model, and audit, in the beta branch of the nervud repository. We also won the Best Explorer Award at Songling, the Second Prize in Future Intelligence-viaim, and the Third Prize in the Kaleidoscope Rainbow Track during this event.
But after all, there were only 5 days, so a lot of code was generated by AI. In order to better manage the operating system development and also to enhance stability, we decided to rewrite the entire architecture. After the prototype was finalized on August 11th, we went through the entire project again, organized a complete architecture document, and created a Nervus-Console to manage this project.
September 26th started the rewrite. Currently, it's the kernel skeleton: configuration loading, event bus, component registry, Unix Domain Socket communication layer. During the rewrite process, we mainly do it manually with AI assistance, and each logic block is written after thoroughly thinking through it.

**Next Research Direction**
In addition to continuing with NervusOS, we want to try applying liquid neural networks to robotic motion control. This also aligns with our unified AI loading idea.
Robot control is inherently a dynamics problem in continuous time, and the state evolution of liquid networks is also in continuous time. It does not guess the next frame at a fixed frame rate, but rather solves continuously along the time axis. MIT CSAIL has published research materials on this. We want to train a model truly designed for a robot's body, rather than forcing a general-purpose large model to fit.
